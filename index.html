The mission

The city that put the world on wheels
trains the kids who'll run the automated world.

Detroit Automation Academy exists for one reason: the young people of this city should be the ones building the automated future, not watching it arrive from somewhere else. We teach robotics, drones, and automation the way Detroit taught the world manufacturing — hands on, no shortcuts, pride in the work.

This academy has never received institutional funding. No grant, no sponsor, no check that was promised. It was built anyway — nights, weekends, borrowed space — and it's being finished on principle. Detroit's kids shouldn't have to wait on paperwork.

Build, don't watch

Every student leaves with something they made move, fly, or think.

No pity, no jargon

We talk to young builders like builders. High bar, plain language.

Detroit first

The talent is here. The training should be too.

The Ride Is Free

Published September 30, 2026

A new series. Same newsletter. What does Detroit look like in a hundred years if we build it for everyone who'll live here — not just everyone who can pay today? First up: how we move.

  • On August 4, 67% of Wayne County voters — 230,387 people — approved a countywide transit millage. All 43 communities are in, including the 17 that had opted out of SMART.
  • Free transit works: Luxembourg's tram went from 6 million to over 31 million annual riders; Kansas City's fare-free years ran 24% above peer-city ridership. Funding it forever is the hard part.
  • The 100-year version of the Wolverine corridor: electrified, hourly, Detroit–Chicago in about two and a half hours — 231 of the 304 miles are already publicly owned.

Thirty Days to Keep the Doors Open

Published September 29, 2026

An off-cycle edition. The Detroit People's Food Co-op — a 15,000-square-foot, community-owned grocery store in the North End — made its situation public: a fundraising push to keep the doors open, with about a month on the clock.

  • Roughly $3 million in annual sales, solid wages, prices in line with the big chains — and still struggling against rising costs and a model the neighborhood misunderstands.
  • Anyone can walk in and buy food — no membership, no extra charge. Membership is something else: ownership. A vote. A committee seat. A share of the profits, if there are any.
  • Three ways in: donate to the GoFundMe campaign (live through November 30), invest ($1,000 minimum), or show up — the store is open daily, 8am to 8pm.

The Signal · Edition 1

BIOBUZZ Is Here: What the New FTC Season Demands

Published September 23, 2026

The 2026-27 FIRST Tech Challenge game is BIOBUZZ. How the game scores, the six rule changes that matter, and why this year's rulebook rewards teams that build for the spirit of the rule.

  • The game: hives at field center (20 points per tip), flowers that only score in the last 60 seconds, gardens in the corners that pay 1 point per element — and can be stolen from.
  • Six rule changes worth coaching: four elements max, no camping under the hive, flowers gated to the final minute, one legal way to tip a hive, the human player as a real position, autonomous that pays.
  • The real change is philosophical: the rulebook now emphasizes the spirit of the rule. Don't hunt loopholes — build for what the rule protects.
